### Response:

### Instruction:

### Prethink:
This is a circumscribed circle (circumcircle) problem for triangle ABC, where O is the circumcenter, \(OB = OC = 2.0\) (radius), and \(\angle BAC = 60.0^\circ\). To find the length of BC, we first use the circumferential angle theorem to get the central angle corresponding to BC, then apply the law of cosines (or properties of equilateral triangles) in \(\triangle OBC\).

### Response:
Let's think step by step.
Step 1: Use the circumferential angle theorem: The central angle corresponding to an arc is twice the circumferential angle.Since \(\angle BAC = 60.0^\circ\) (circumferential angle for arc BC), the central angle \(\angle BOC = 2 \times 60.0^\circ = 120.0^\circ\).
Step 2: Analyze \(\triangle OBC\): \(OB = OC = 2.0\) (radii), so \(\triangle OBC\) is isosceles with \(OB = OC = 2.0\) and \(\angle BOC = 120.0^\circ\).
Step 3: Calculate BC using the law of cosines:\(BC^2 = OB^2 + OC^2 - 2 \times OB \times OC \times \cos\angle BOC\),\(BC^2 = 2^2 + 2^2 - 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times \cos120^\circ\),Since \(\cos120^\circ = -\frac{1}{2}\):\(BC^2 = 4 + 4 - 8 \times \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) = 8 + 4 = 12\),Thus, \(BC = \sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3} \approx 3.46\)
Step 4: The answer is \(2\sqrt{3}\) (or approximately 3.46).
